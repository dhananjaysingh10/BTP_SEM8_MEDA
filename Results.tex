All numbers in this chapter come from a single deterministic run of the full benchmark suite ($n = 323$) with beam-search width $K = 3$ for LARP and ILP, and the dilution-aware subtree cost ($w_s = 1000$, $w_d = 5$). Results are reproduced via \texttt{node bench.js} from a fresh clone.

\textbf{A note on methodology.} An earlier iteration of this work used a 7-metric composite to rank algorithms per test. On audit, three sources of inflation were identified:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item $\text{leaves} = \text{mixes} + 1$ is mathematically locked in any binary tree, so leaves and mixes are the same metric counted twice.
    \item \textit{splits} is highly correlated with \textit{mixes} in the test suite (per-test win-rate vectors are identical for both).
    \item \textit{depth} is invariant across all algorithms because it is determined by the input.
\end{enumerate}
After correction, the headline is reported on \textbf{4 independent metrics}: $m$, $l$, $\text{maxL}$, $p$, supplemented with sole-vs-tied wins, per-rank distribution, and pairwise tie matrix.

Key observations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item LARP and ILP \textbf{strictly improve over RMA} on size and parallelism (5 of 6 metrics) at the cost of dilution-chain length.
    \item LARP and ILP \textbf{strictly improve over BS} on dilution structure (2 of 2 dilution metrics) at the cost of 6--10\% more mix operations.
    \item Neither LARP nor ILP dominates either baseline; they occupy a \textbf{previously unfilled middle} of the splits-vs-dilution trade-off curve.
\end{itemize}
